5 did not move — which is the real story

A frozen threshold is a tax rise that never has to be announced. Pay rises with inflation, the threshold does not, and a larger slice of every wage crosses into the next band. The Treasury calls it fiscal drag.

Scotland moves separately. Income tax is devolved, so Scottish bands change on their own timetable and Scotland has six of them against England's three. National Insurance is reserved and applies UK-wide unchanged. See the Scottish income tax calculator for what that means on a salary.

What it costs you

These are thresholds, not outcomes. To see what the year's changes do to an actual wage, the take-home pay calculator applies all of them at once, and the fiscal drag calculator isolates what the frozen thresholds alone have cost you since they were pegged.

If a student loan threshold above is the line that matters, the student loan repayment calculator covers all five plans, and the pay rise calculator shows how much of a raise survives the bands.
